Part A · Errors to fix

[P1] Photosynthesis and Respiration (POGIL worksheet)

E1

Q9: the energy exception is in the wrong half

[P1] p.2, Q9 · Science (half right) · Medium priority

You wrote“The reactants of photosynthesis are the products of respiration, except for sunlight energy, and the reactants of respiration are the products of photosynthesis, except for ATP energy.”
What's offThe first half is right. In the second half there is no exception: glucose + O2 (respiration’s reactants) are exactly what photosynthesis makes. ATP is a product of respiration, not one of its reactants, so “except for ATP” suggests ATP goes into respiration.
Full-credit version“The products of respiration (CO2 and H2O) are the reactants of photosynthesis, and the products of photosynthesis (glucose and O2) are the reactants of respiration. The only difference is energy: photosynthesis takes in light energy, and respiration releases energy as ATP.”
Why it matters on a CER testEnergy is the #1 trap on this test: matter cycles, but energy does not. Getting “light in, ATP out” exactly right shows you understand both processes.
E2

Q10: animals DO have mitochondria

[P1] p.2, Q10 · Contradiction / science error · High priority

You wrote“…but animals can only go through respiration on their own because they don’t have mitochondria.”
What's offAnimals do have mitochondria — that is where their cellular respiration happens (you said so yourself in Q2a and Q8). You almost certainly meant chloroplasts. Also, “plants can survive on their own” is only partly true: plants still use CO2 released by respiration (their own, animals’, and decomposers’), and decomposers recycle matter.
Full-credit version“We partly agree. Plants have chloroplasts and mitochondria, so they can make their own glucose and also break it down for ATP. Animals have mitochondria but no chloroplasts, so they must eat plants (or animals that ate plants) to get glucose. But plants aren’t completely independent: they use CO2 released by the respiration of animals and decomposers, so the two depend on each other.”
Why it matters on a CER testA one-word slip (mitochondria ↔ chloroplasts) turns a correct idea into a science error and contradicts your own earlier answers. On a CER test, a contradiction can cost the reasoning points.

Q14: the energy labels are swapped

[P1] p.4, Q14 · Science error · High priority

You wroteRespiration: C6H12O6 + 6O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O + energy, with “(sunlight)” written over “energy”. Photosynthesis: 6CO2 + 6H2O + energy → C6H12O6 + 6O2, with “(ATP)” written over “energy”.
What's offThe two labels are switched. Photosynthesis takes in light (sunlight) energy; respiration releases energy as ATP. This also contradicts your own correct answers to Q1b (“sunlight energy”) and Q2c (“ATP energy”). Your formulas, coefficients and circled CO2 are all correct.
Full-credit versionPhotosynthesis: 6CO2 + 6H2O + light energy → C6H12O6 + 6O2. Respiration: C6H12O6 + 6O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O + energy (ATP). “Label” also means naming each substance: carbon dioxide, water, glucose, oxygen, energy.
Why it matters on a CER testThis is the single most likely trap on the test. If your reasoning says respiration uses sunlight, the science is wrong and the reasoning points are lost.

E10

Board meeting: enzymes don’t denature just above 20 °C

[P2] p.5, Part VI table (temperature) · Science · Medium priority

You wrote“~20 °C … At higher temps (>20 °C), enzymes, proteins to speed up chemical reactions, are denatured.”
What's offEnzymes don’t fall apart just above 20 °C. Above the best temperature they work less efficiently (they start to lose their shape), and the plant’s respiration speeds up, using more O2 — so the net oxygen drops. True denaturing of most plant enzymes needs much higher temperatures (roughly 40 °C or more). Also, only 15, 20, 25, 30 and 35 °C were tested, so 20 °C is the best tested temperature.
Full-credit version“About 20 °C gave the most O2 of the temperatures tested. Colder: molecules move and collide more slowly, so enzyme reactions are slow. Warmer: enzymes work less well and the plant’s respiration uses more O2. Very hot (about 40 °C or more): enzymes denature.”
Why it matters on a CER test“Denature” is a strong word; using it at the wrong temperature is a science error that can cost the reasoning point.

[P3] Notes: Mauna Loa & the Rise of CO2

E13

Notes: “photosynthesis is a delayed reaction”

[P3] p.1 (starred note) · Science (partly right) · Medium priority

You wrote“Photosynthesis takes time to take CO2 from the atmosphere, which is why … there is more CO2 in the atmosphere in the spring than in the fall. Photosynthesis is a delayed reaction.”
What's offPartly right! CO2 really is higher in spring (peak in May) than in fall (low in September–October), and the CO2 level does lag behind the seasons. But photosynthesis itself isn’t delayed — it starts within seconds of light. The lag happens because the CO2 level is a running total, like water in a bathtub (Figure 6).
Full-credit version“All fall and winter, respiration and decay add more CO2 than photosynthesis removes, so CO2 keeps rising until the leaves come back — it peaks in May. Then months of strong photosynthesis remove more than respiration adds, so CO2 falls until its low point in September–October.”
Why it matters on a CER testKeeling-curve questions are a favorite “explain the pattern” CER. The running-total idea is the reasoning teachers look for.
E14

Notes: respiration is NOT negligible

[P3] p.1 · Science · High priority

You wrote“Cellular respiration is a constant factor, so it’s happening all the time, which is why it has a negligible effect on CO2 in the atmosphere.”
What's offRespiration by plants, animals and especially decomposers releases a huge amount of CO2 — about as much as photosynthesis removes in a year. That is exactly the balance you described in POGIL Q18. It is what makes CO2 rise every fall and winter. Something that happens all the time still adds CO2 all the time. (The lesson probably meant: because respiration goes on all year, the big season-to-season change comes mostly from photosynthesis turning up and down.)
Full-credit version“Respiration happens all year and releases about as much CO2 as photosynthesis removes. When photosynthesis slows in fall and winter, respiration and decay keep going, so CO2 rises. The seasonal zig-zag comes mainly from photosynthesis changing with the seasons.”
Why it matters on a CER testThis note contradicts your own Q15 (decomposers respire) and Q18 (balance). Mock Question Q11 asks you to rebut exactly this claim.
